Written by Royce Williams, former employee of the US Army Corps of Engineers (USACE), documenting institutional misconduct across the Huntington, Southwestern, and Detroit Districts from 2022 to 2026.
A Chronicle in Eight Acts

The Tale of a Scribe Cast Adrift

Wherein a humble keeper of ledgers traverses three realms, exposes the rot beneath the marble, and is rewarded with forged ink and phantom signatures.

— Of House USACE · A.D. MMXXII – MMXXVI —
Let the chronicle unfold
Act the First

The Mountain Hall

Anno MMXXII · The Green Wilds of West Virginia

In the second year after the great plague, I pledged my quill to House USACE, and was sent to the mountain hall in the green wilds of West Virginia. There I found a council of elders — gray of beard, slow of mind — who clung to ancient scrolls and forgotten rites as though the rituals themselves were sacred.

Their processes were crumbling parchment. Words once meaningful, now mere ceremony. The young scribes labored beneath burdens shaped by men long since departed, and the suffering passed from generation to generation as though it were a holy inheritance.

I bore it. For a season, I bore it. But the rot was already showing through the gilding.

Act the Second

The Breaking

Anno MMXXIV · The First Resignation

For two winters I bore the weight. But on the eve of the third spring, I could bear it no more. I laid down my quill upon the table of the elders. I walked from the mountain hall with my honor intact, and the dust of their bureaucracy shaken clean from my boots.

Some called it surrender. I called it refusal. There is a difference, and history has always known it.

Act the Third

The Northern Crossing

Anno MMXXIV · The Great Lakes · House Detroit

The Great Lakes called. The whispers spake of a different house — House Detroit — where operations were the lifeblood of the realm, and where a scribe of vision might serve a higher purpose than ritual.

So I journeyed north. I pledged anew. And for a brief and golden season, I believed the new banner I served beneath.

Act the Fourth

The Numbers That Spoke Truth

In the Halls of Detroit

In the halls of Detroit, I laid open the ledgers. I traced gold to deed, and deed to outcome. Where my predecessors had spent gold for the mere sake of spending — that the coffers might be refilled the following year — I dared to ask the question that none had spoken aloud:

What have we built? What have we earned? Was the gold well-spent, or merely spent?

The schedulers in their distant chambers grew restless. The analysts in their dim offices began to whisper. For a scribe who ties gold to truth is a scribe who threatens the comfortable rot — and the comfortable rot, dear reader, does not suffer threats kindly.

Act the Fifth

A Threat Wearing a Quill

When Merit Became a Sin

They had named me an ally. Now they named me a danger. Not because I had failed — but because I had succeeded. Not because I broke the work — but because I exposed how broken the work had been before I arrived.

The lords of seats and chairs descended from on high. Positions came under scrutiny. And rather than weigh men by their merit, the council of House Detroit chose to weigh men by the familiar warmth of their faces.

I was unfamiliar. I was inconvenient. I was, above all, correct — and that, in the politics of a dying institution, is the most unforgivable crime of all.

Act the Sixth

The False Quest

The Conspiracy of the Crooked Ink

And so the conspiracy was struck. A summons came: a hundred and twenty days of service to the western kingdom of San Francisco. A noble quest, said they. An opportunity, said they. A chance to broaden the scribe's horizons.

But I had read the scroll. I had seen the ink — crooked, unsteady, drawn by hands not whose names it bore. The seal was forged. The signatures were ghosts. The bargain had been struck in shadow chambers, and the price of my passage was my exile.

I knew from the very first hour what they had planned. They believed me blind. I was not.

Act the Seventh

The Western Court

The City by the Bay

San Francisco received me. And in that western kingdom, my methods bore fruit. The lords there saw what Detroit had refused to see — that the scribe had value, that the ledger could be read truly, that the gold could be governed with wisdom rather than wasted with custom.

They learned from me. They took what I offered freely. They built upon my work.

But the bargain in the shadows held its weight, even there. I was a man already cast out, even as I labored. I was a vessel they would empty, then return to the eastern sea. Such was the deal. Such had always been the deal.

Act the Eighth · The Final Betrayal

The Homecoming Trial

The Phantom's Signature

I returned to the Great Lakes. Not in hope — but in trial. I came to see whether the promise of return — the right plainly inked upon the cursed SF-50 — would hold its weight in the light of day.

I came to test the conscience of House Detroit. To witness, with my own eyes, what manner of men they had become.

They did not disappoint. They signed the final scroll. And the name upon it belonged to no living soul. A phantom's mark. A signature without a man. A countersign drawn by no human hand.

The betrayal was complete. And it was witnessed.

— Epilogue —

The Quill, Laid Down

And so I lay down my quill.

Not in defeat. Not in shame. But in clarity — for I have seen the rot, and I have named it. I have suffered the schemes, and I have survived them.

The realm shall do what realms always do: it shall forget. But the ledgers — the ledgers shall remember. For they cannot lie.

Justice for Royce

The maesters of House USACE were gifted a scribe of uncommon loyalty.
They repaid him with banishment.

It is a rare talent — to destroy a man
without ever unsheathing a blade.
They had that talent in abundance.

First they moved him. Then they buried him. Then they forged the hand
that signed away what the law had plainly promised.
A phantom's countersign. Witnessed. Documented. Remembered.

In the great tradition of scheming lordlings everywhere:
they blamed the ledger-keeper for the ledger's contents.

Three realms. Eight acts. One man standing.
The realm may yet forget.
But the ledgers — as ever — shall not.
